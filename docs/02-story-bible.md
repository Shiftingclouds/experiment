# 02 — Story Bible: *HELDWATER*

> *What is kept, keeps.* — motto carved over the Oathing Hall

## The one-line pitch

You are a first-year at a college built **inside a dam**. The dam is held up by magic, and the magic is
held up by one woman who has not left the building in forty-one years. She is dying. This year's
"Crown" (the College's most coveted honor) goes to whoever will take her place, and almost nobody
knows it.

## The thematic question

**Who should have to hold things up — and is it still a sacrifice if someone chooses it?**

Every ending answers this differently: one person bound forever, a volunteer, a coward redeemed, a
city forced to pay what it owes, a flood, or *many hands, each holding a little*.

## Tone

Warm, funny, specific, humane, and then devastating. It should read like a real place with laundry,
debt, homesickness, bad dinners and good friends, and like a school where the building might kill everyone
below it. The magic is intimate and costly. Nobody is a cartoon: the "villains" have good arguments and the
"saints" are angry.

---

## The world

### Scarrow
An industrial river city at the mouth of the **Aske** valley. It has dye-works, mills and a canal called the Cut, with
lock-keepers, trams and a daily paper (*the Scarrow Courier*).

| District | Notes |
|---|---|
| **The Nethers** | The lowest wards along the river. Poor and crowded, and **first to drown** if the dam fails. |
| **The Rows** (Hebble Rows) | The terraces inside the Nethers where Hebble's survivors were resettled. |
| **Mill Reach**, **Old Market** | Working city. |
| **Crowhill** | The wealthy heights, **above the flood line**. The whole Council lives on Crowhill. Draw your own conclusions. |

Swears and idioms: *"Drown it."* *"Drowned hell."* *"Sweet Stay."* *"On my word."* Scarrow folk grow up under
the dam's hum and stop hearing it by adulthood.

### The Stay
A colossal curved stone **arch dam**, four hundred feet high, built **seventy-one years ago** in a single
season by **Josiah Thwaite**, Master-Sworn. It is structurally inadequate. The stone is too thin for the
water it holds, and **magic does the rest**. It hums in **B-flat**. When the note drops, something is
wrong. The dam "weeps" through its weep-holes, and students log the seepage.

The **College of the Stay** was founded with the dam to train the Sworn who maintain it. It occupies
the dam's interior galleries and the buildings along its crest. It has about 130 students aged
18–22, taught over four years. The Oathing Act forbids vows before eighteen.

Students are divided into four **Watches** (in place of "houses"), named for parts of the dam: **Crest**,
**Sluice**, **Gallery**, and **Footing**. Footing Watch is the damp, pitied dorm at the very base, where the
hum is loudest. Watches do real maintenance patrols. You are in Footing Watch.

Places: the **Weir Lift** (a funicular up the downstream face), the **Oathing Hall** and the **Throat**
(a shaft into the dam's heart where vows are spoken), the **Great Hall** (with Thwaite's bronze statue;
students rub its nose for luck), the **Sump** (Footing Watch's common room), the **lower galleries**, the
sealed **Hebble Gallery**, the **Keystone Chamber** at the crown of the arch, the **Old Sluices**
(sealed decades ago for the mills' convenience), and the **crest** with its braziers, wind and beehives.

### The Heldwater and Hebble
The reservoir behind the Stay is eleven miles of cold, deep water. Under it lies **Hebble**, a valley
village of 300 souls. It was drowned when the Stay was built. In dry summers its Wordhouse spire
breaks the surface. On still nights people swear they hear its bell.

**The truth the city has buried:** Hebble did not "consent to resettlement." Thwaite could not hold the
Stay alone while it was built, so the Aldermen compelled **206 Hebble villagers to swear the Holding**, a
collective vow ("*I will not leave the valley*") that anchored the dam through a great iron lattice in what
is now the sealed Hebble Gallery. They were held for **thirty years**. They were confined to the Rows,
and many died of "Holding lung," a wasting sickness from the strain. **Forty-one years ago** the College's
second Warden, Ingram Sallis, redesigned the binding to rest on **one volunteer**, the **Keystone**. That
released the survivors. The first and only Keystone is **Hester Quaile**, who volunteered at nineteen.

Thirty-one years ago the Council's *Aske Valley Settlement* declared Hebble "compensated in full and
resettled with its consent." That erased the Holding from the official record.

### The Keeping
Scarrow's folk faith: *keep your word and the world keeps you.* Its chapels are called Wordhouses.

---

## The magic: vows and purchase

- **A vow** is a lifelong restriction you accept in your own words, in the form *"I will not ___."*
- In exchange you gain **purchase**, leverage on the world. The greater the vow's cost *to you*,
  the more purchase you get. A teetotaller swearing off drink gains nothing.
- Purchase is **flavored** by the vow. A vow against lying gives purchase over truth. A vow against violence
  gives purchase to hold and protect. A vow against refusing help gives purchase over ways and doors. A vow
  of self-effacement gives purchase over being unnoticed.
- Using purchase is called **leaning**. *"She leaned on the door and it opened."* It tires you, like
  breath, and it recovers.
- Breaking a vow is **the snap**. All that purchase recoils at once, bringing pain, injury and sometimes
  death, and you can never swear that vow again. Snapping a vow also releases a **surge**: one huge
  burst of purchase, if you are willing to bleed for it.
- **Cradle-swearing**, a parent swearing a vow on a baby's behalf, has been illegal since the Oathing Act.
  Old families still do it.
- **Hedge-swearing** means vowing outside a Wordhouse or the College, unwitnessed and unregistered.
  It is illegal for under-eighteens. It is common in the Rows.
- Every registered vow is recorded with the Council. Under the Oathing Act the Council holds the
  **Writ of Restraint**, an iron seal that, in an emergency, **freezes every registered Sworn in sight**.
  The unsworn and the unregistered cannot be held. *They hold you by your word.*
- **The Great Vow** is *"I will not leave the Stay."* It is sworn into the Keystone lattice. It costs a whole
  life, so it gives enough purchase to hold the dam. The younger the swearer, the longer the lever, which is
  why they choose the young.
- **The Holding** is the old many-person lattice. It can hold the Stay on many *small* vows instead of one
  enormous one. It was sealed because a hundred people are harder to control than one.

## The Crown

Every year since the Keystone was created, students have competed in three **Crown trials**: *the
Gallery* (autumn), *the Question* (winter) and *the Deep* (spring). The winner is **Crowned at
Midsummer** as **Heir to the Stay**. The Crown is a great honor and a famous career launch, and most
Crowned go on to the Council or the Bench. Its literal meaning is that the Crowned stands ready to replace
the Keystone. That has been called on **only once**. Twenty-two years ago Ambrose Fell was Crowned, the
Keystone faltered in the Wet Winter, and Fell *ran*. Hester deepened her own vow to hold on, and it cost
her sight.

**This year Hester is dying.** The Council has a buried engineering report: the Stay has fourteen to twenty
months without reinforcement or drawdown. Whoever is Crowned this Midsummer *will be bound*.

## Cast

### The player
A first-year in **Footing Watch**. They choose name, pronouns (he / she / they) and background.

| Background | Who you are | What it gives you |
|---|---|---|
| **Levy Scholar** (the Nethers) | Raised under the dam. Your Mam is a lock-keeper on the Cut. Your little brother **Pip** (10) is the light of your life. The city paid your way; you owe it service. | Stakes in the flood zone. Street sense (+Nerve, +Sway). Mam can sound the canal sirens. |
| **Crowhill Legacy** | An old Sworn family. Your mother, **Advocate Judith**, is counsel to the Council's Stay Committee. Your parents *refused* to cradle-swear you, a quiet scandal. You've known Tolly since dancing lessons. | Access to Council papers. Book-learning (+Lore, +Finesse). A parent implicated in the cover-up. |
| **Lisk Outsider** | From across the Strait, where oath-craft is sin. At eight, at your father's funeral, you swore without knowing it: ***"I will not weep."*** It took. You have not cried since. | A wild vow (+Purchase, +Bold). Fresh eyes. The chance to hide or declare the vow, and at the climax, to weep. |

### Footing Watch

**Tamsin Mottram** (she/her, 19): *the rival.* From the Hebble Rows. She worked three years in the dye-works
before passing the Levy exam, and works nights in the College laundry. She is **hedge-sworn at fourteen:
*"I will take nothing I have not earned."*** She physically cannot accept charity, including kindness
that feels like charity, and eventually including love. Her voice is clipped and dry, precise, with rare
and devastating tenderness. She wears her Nan's **rusted house-key** on a string: a key to a house forty
fathoms down. **Her secret plan:** win the Crown, become Keystone, and use the Keystone's control of the
Stay to lower the lake slowly over years and give Hebble back, with herself as the prisoner who does it.
*Trope treatment:* the rival is not the rich snob but the poorest person in the building. Her "villain plan"
is a sacrifice, and your goals might be the same.
*Romanceable.* Rivals to lovers, where love has to get past a vow that says she must earn everything.

**Ptolemy "Tolly" Varnish** (he/him, 18): *the best friend with a chain.* Crowhill money, baroque sentences,
catastrophic charm. **Cradle-sworn at one year old by his mother: *"I will not disobey my mother."*** Every
command she gives, his body obeys before he decides. His purchase is enormous and it only works when he is
obeying her. His mother is **Councillor Honoria Varnish**, who chairs the Stay Committee.
*Trope treatment:* the posh friend is not a Draco but the most trapped person in the school. His comedy is a
survival strategy.
*Arc:* commanded to enter the Crown for appearances, commanded to lose, and later commanded to *spy on
you*. Can he be freed, and by whom?
*Romanceable.*

**Sal Quaile** (they/them, 18): *the true believer.* Hester's grand-nibling, raised visiting the Keystone
Chamber. Beekeeper, with hives on the crest. Devout in the Keeping. **Sworn to the Plain Word: they cannot
lie**, so ask them anything. Calm and literal, with sudden oblique jokes. **They want to be the next
Keystone because they love Hester and think her life is holy.** They know exactly what it means.
*Trope treatment:* the sacrificial innocent is not naive. The story has to ask whether a clear-eyed volunteer
is still a victim.
*Romanceable.*

**Hob Gorringe** (he/him, 21): Footing Watch captain, a fourth-year. Enormous, soft-spoken, and an
engineering obsessive who explains everything in terms of load paths. He knows every gallery.

### Faculty

**Warden Agnes Brathwaite** (63): head of the College. She is **sworn to the Plain Word**, so she *cannot lie*.
She also carries a vow she swore as the price of her appointment: ***"I will not speak of the Stay's condition
beyond the Board."*** She won't lie to you, but she can refuse to answer, and her silence can be the answer.
Terse, exact and formidable. She has fought the Council for nineteen years and lost. She has grimly let
the Crown proceed rather than let the dam fall, and she is secretly hoping someone finds a better way.
*Trope treatment:* the mysterious headmaster is the most honest person in the story. The mystery survives
only because nobody asks her the right question.

**Master Ambrose Fell** (44): teaches Practical Leaning. **The Crowned who ran.** The snap from breaking his
Crown oath burned out his purchase, and he has sworn nothing since. He is **unsworn**. He is gentle, rambling,
compulsively tidy, keeps an eel called Magistrate in a tank, and is ashamed to his bones.
*Trope treatment:* the wise mentor who dies is here a coward who lives. His moment is whether, twenty-two years
late, he stays.

**Dr. Odile Marchbank** (50s): teaches Water & Stone (engineering). Blunt and numerical. **She wrote the
buried report**, and the Council made her swear silence: *"I will not publish the report."* She cannot publish
it. She can leave it on her desk and step out for eleven minutes. She knows the Old Sluices.

**Mr. Quentin Ebbing** (60s): archivist, teaches Oath Law & History. Pompous, timid, and secretly brave. He has
been compiling the true history of Hebble for twenty years and never dared publish it.

### Beyond the College

**Hester Quaile** (60): **the Keystone**, bound at nineteen, forty-one years ago. She volunteered in order to free
the 206. She is blind, having given her sight in the Wet Winter to cover Fell's flight. She sings to hold the Stay,
and the whole building hears it. She is salty, funny and frank, a dockworker's daughter turned poet of the stone.
**Furious underneath**, and never allowed to say so. She can feel the whole dam, and she can hear the 206 voices
still in its walls. She has not smelled rain in forty-one years.
*Trope treatment:* the sacrificed saint is angry. She will say *"I would do it again"* and *"I hate them for
asking"* in the same breath, and mean both.

**Winifred "Nan Win" Mottram** (88): Tamsin's grandmother. **The youngest of the 206**, who swore the Holding at
seventeen. Warm, funny and lethal. She makes soup at three in the morning because ***she has not slept in
thirty-one years***. She swore ***"I will not sleep while the Stay stands"*** at her husband's grave the week the
Settlement erased Hebble. **She is the saboteur.** Every night she knits a white jumper and unravels it again,
and her lean runs up the river water into the Stay's stone, *unpicking* it. That is why the Stay is failing
faster than age explains. She knows it is killing Hester, the woman who freed her. She is sorry every night,
and every night she pulls another thread.
*Trope treatment:* the dark lord is a sweet old woman with an unanswerable grievance and an unforgivable method.

**Councillor Honoria Varnish**: chair of the Stay Committee and Tolly's mother. Velvet, reasonable, and never
raises her voice. She believes she is the only adult in the room: *"A new dam costs the city a generation.
The Keystone costs one volunteer, freely given, and honored. I am sorry it is so. I did not make the world."*
She buried the report. She holds the Writ.
*Trope treatment:* the villain you can't punch. She has spreadsheets.

**Aurelia "Rilla" Hesketh** (21): Crest Watch, fourth-year, golden favorite to win the Crown. Kind to
first-years, and ambitious for a seat on the Council. She does not know what the Crown means. If she learns
it she withdraws at once, which is the sane response, and she can bring half the College with her.

**Mr. Ezra Dunnock**: schoolteacher in the Rows and secretary of the peaceful Hebble Society, which files
petitions and keeps a phone tree.

**Pip** (10): your little brother, if you are a Levy Scholar. He draws dams.

**Old Samuel**: runs the Weir Lift, and has done for thirty years.

## How the tropes are treated

| Trope | How *Heldwater* handles it |
|---|---|
| The Chosen One | Being "chosen" is a life sentence, and the prize everyone is competing for. The last act argues that **no one** should be chosen. |
| The Sorting Hat / Houses | Watches are work crews. You get the worst one, and it's the best. |
| The Rival | The poorest student, not the richest. Her scheme is self-sacrifice. Rivals to lovers, complicated by a vow. |
| The Rich Friend | Literally bound to obey his mother. His comedy covers a cage. |
| The Wise Mentor Who Dies | The mentor is a coward who lives. His arc is whether he finally stays. |
| The Mysterious Headmaster | She cannot lie. The secret survives because no one asks the right question. |
| The Tournament | Harry Potter's Triwizard Tournament turned inside out. The prize is the danger. Seconds are loyal friends, and you can throw a race to save someone. |
| The Dark Lord | An 88-year-old survivor who hasn't slept in 31 years, killing the woman who freed her. |
| The Sacrificial Saint | Furious. Also at peace. Both at once. |
| The Institutional Villain | Reasonable, budget-driven, and never raises her voice. |
| The Forbidden Section | Sealed because the truth is embarrassing, not dangerous. |
| Power Fantasy | Every bit of power costs a freedom. The unsworn student, the powerless one, may be the only person who can move at the climax. |
| Magic Has A Price | The price is a promise you have to keep forever, and breaking it is the most powerful thing you can do. |

## Motifs

- **The hum** (B-flat). It drops when things are wrong.
- **Hester's singing** heard through the stone.
- **Nan's key** to a house forty fathoms down.
- **Rain.** Hester hasn't felt it in 41 years. Crown Night brings a storm.
- **The knitting** that is made and unmade every night (Penelope's loom).
- **Bees**, which keep the hive together, each holding a little.
- **The drowned bell** of Hebble.
